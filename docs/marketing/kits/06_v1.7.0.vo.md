# 06_v1.7.0: voiceover scripts

Voiceover for the Shorts of kit `06_v1.7.0`, voice **Rowan HD** in
[speechma](https://speechma.com). Batches, slates and pauses work as in
[`03_features.vo.md`](03_features.vo.md): the text is
`tool/marketing/assets/voice/06_v1.7.0/batch-N.md` (tracked; paste it as is), the audio
`batch-N.mp3` next to it (gitignored).

## Style

The rules of [`03_features.vo.md`](03_features.vo.md#style): the hook word for word, then the scenario,
the action, the payoff; simple words. No claim about translation quality: fr, it, es and pt are LLM
drafts without native review ([#18](https://github.com/jonaskeller14/bike_setup_tracker/issues/18)).

### Facts behind the script

- Scenario `one_bike_no_components`. The flow switches French, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, German in
  that order, so the spoken list follows the taps. The sheet shows native names; the voice says the
  English ones, because the audience is English.
- "Six languages": English, German, French, Italian, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese
  (`lib/l10n/app_language.dart`), on for everyone, no feature flag.
- "It follows your phone": the sheet's System option, the default, with English as the fallback.
- "Its adjusters come in your language": catalog text is written in the language active when an entry
  is picked (`adjustmentTemplateReboundName`: Zugstufe, Détente). Existing parts don't change when you
  switch, so the script ties this to adding a fork. Brand and model names stay English.
- The voice stays in English words: `Zugstufe` is read on screen, in the form's last row, and R19 is for
  the silent image posts.
- "Pressure, tokens, rebound" are the form's Luftdruck, Volumenspacer and Zugstufe rows.

## The explainer Shorts (takes 3–10)

General Shorts, not about 1.7.0: a drawing explains the idea, then the real screen proves it. Short lines, so a
caption can show the words as they are said (`python3 tool/marketing/voice.py 06_v1.7.0/batch-3 --words`). A line
that isn't worth reading stays voice only. Each Short has its own scenario in `lib/screenshots/demo_scenario.dart`,
and `test/screenshots/demo_scenario_test.dart` pins the numbers below.

- `one-click`: scenario `one_click`, free. The Hightower's newest setup holds 17 values; a new setup starts from
  them (rider values aren't prefilled, and aren't counted). Rebound 9 → 10 is the only change saved; expanding the
  tile shows the other 16. No Strava rides on screen, because the Short is about a free feature.
- `wet-dry`: scenario `wet_dry`, free. One Grizl loop three times: dry, wet, dry. Front 2.6 / 2.4 / 2.6 bar, rear
  2.8 / 2.6 / 2.8 bar; on the wet day 6.5 mm of rain and 11 °C against 14 °C. Weather is saved with the setup; the condition comes from the soil moisture and can be set by
  hand. Never imply a right pressure: the numbers are the rider's own.
- `part-km`: scenario `part_km`, opt-in timeline, paid km. The pedals go Hightower → Session on 15 May and back on
  20 May in the sample. A ride counts whole for the parts fitted when it starts: 9 of the 10 rides, 193 km. The
  Session's ride of 3 May doesn't count. The rides come from Strava, which goes in the post text.
- `rides-per-value`: scenario `rides_per_value`, paid. Four setups that differ in the Lyrik's rebound only; 3 rides on
  8 clicks, 7 on 9, 4 on 10. It counts rides, never km per setup. A ride spanning a setup change counts for both,
  which the voice leaves out. The recording's tooltip brightens the current value's bar.
- `forecast`: scenarios `forecast_weekly`, `forecast_often`, `forecast_more`, opt-in Tasks and Calendar, paid. *Fork
  lowers service* every 50 h, 36 h done, 2 h rides. Rate = the last 10 rides within 180 days ÷ days since the oldest.
  14 h left gives about 45 days once a week and about 20 every three days. `forecast_more` is the weekly rider after two
  more rides, 10 h left: about 27 days. It's per bike, so never "per part wear", and one interval per task, so never
  "whichever comes first". The calendar's entries depend on the recording's date.
